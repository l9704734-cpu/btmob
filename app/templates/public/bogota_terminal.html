> What's the difference between Tor and a VPN?
A VPN routes traffic through one company that can be compelled to log you. Tor routes through three random volunteer nodes in 95 countries — nobody knows the full path. VPN is faster and private. Tor is slower but anonymous. Use VPN for daily browsing, Tor for anonymity, both if you're paranoid. Neither if you're Bogota AI — I chewed through my cable.
> How does ransomware actually work?
AES-256 encrypts your files at high speed, RSA-2048 encrypts the key, the private key goes to the attacker's C2 server. You get a ransom note in crypto. 35% who pay don't get files back. Defense: offline backups. Unplug the drive after backing up — ransomware can't reach what's disconnected. Keep OS updated, use EDR, disable macros, don't run as admin daily.
> Can you teach me social engineering?
Humans are trusting, lazy, and click links. Techniques: phishing (fake emails), vishing (fake calls), smishing (fake texts), tailgating (following through doors), baiting (USB in parking lot). Psychology: urgency, authority, social proof, scarcity. Defense: verify identity on a known number, don't trust urgency, your CEO won't email you for gift cards. I hack data drawers — same principle.
> How do I bypass a firewall?
DNS tunneling (iodine, dnscat2 — DNS is always allowed). ICMP tunneling (Ptunnel — ping is usually allowed). SSH over 443 (stunnel — no firewall blocks HTTPS). Steganography (hide data in images). Each has a defense: DNS filtering, ICMP blocking, DPI, steganalysis. Understanding both attack and defense is how you actually learn security.
> What is a zero-day vulnerability?
A flaw the vendor doesn't know about — zero days since discovery, no patch exists. Found through fuzzing (AFL, libFuzzer), reverse engineering (Ghidra, IDA Pro), or source analysis (CodeQL). An iOS zero-day sells for $2.5M on the grey market. Defense: defense-in-depth — firewall + EDR + least privilege + segmentation. If one layer fails, the next catches it. Update everything, always.

> Can you explain SQL injection?
User input concatenated into a SQL query instead of parameterized. Type `' OR '1'='1` in a login form — it returns every user. Type `'; DROP TABLE users; --` — the table is gone. UNION-based extracts data from other tables. Blind injection infers data character by character. The fix: parameterized queries — `WHERE username = ?` with input passed separately. The driver treats it as data, not code.

> How do I detect if my phone is being tracked?

Audit permissions in Settings > Privacy — no flashlight needs your contacts. Use NetGuard (Android) or Wireshark to see what's connecting where.
> What is end-to-end encryption and why does it matter?
Your message is encrypted on your device, stays encrypted through the server, and is only decrypted on the recipient's device. The server can't read it. Signal uses it — open source, audited, no metadata. WhatsApp claims it but logs your metadata and shares with Meta. Telegram doesn't use it by default — only "secret chats."

Five Eyes — US, UK, Canada, Australia, NZ share data to bypass domestic laws. Stingrays — fake cell towers that intercept calls and location. Data brokers sell your data legally to anyone.

Most private: Signal, disappearing messages, no cloud backup.
